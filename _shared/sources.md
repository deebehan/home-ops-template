# sources.md

Exactly what may be read, and who receives the result.

This is the trust document. It is short, and it is the one I would not skip.

An AI with access to your inbox will read your inbox. Not maliciously, just thoroughly. This file is where you say: these three things, and nothing else. It is also the file you show your partner when they ask what you have connected to your email.

## One home for each fact

*This is the rule that stops two calendars quietly disagreeing.*

Every fact lives in exactly one place, and everything else points at it.

- Who is away: [the calendar], not this file
- School events: [the school calendar], not copied by hand into the family one
- The usual week: `household.md`, not the calendar

Copying school events into your own calendar feels tidy and creates a second source of truth that is wrong within a fortnight. Subscribe once, in one place.

## The worked example

> The school calendar is already filtered down to my child's year. Include everything on it. Do not second-guess it, and do not apply any further judgment about whether an event looks relevant.

Without that line, the system re-filters an already-filtered calendar and drops things. Telling it what to trust is as important as telling it what to read.
