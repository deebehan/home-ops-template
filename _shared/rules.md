# rules.md

The judgment, written down.

This is the file that grows. Every other file gets set up once and mostly sits there. This one gets a new line every time the system gets something wrong, and after a couple of months it is the most valuable thing you own, because every line in it is a mistake that cannot happen twice.

Start with these. They are general, they are not mine, and they will hold for any household.

---

## Always

- **Money, forms and bookings go at the top**, in a section called REMEMBER, with the amount and the deadline quoted exactly as the source wrote them.
- **Check for a receipt before asking for money.** If a payment confirmation arrived within a few days of the request, show it as done instead of asking again. Nothing kills trust in a system faster than being nagged for something you paid last week.
- **If a key fact is missing, say so.** "No date published, check the form" beats both omitting it and guessing.
- **Dress-up days, costumes and bring-something days get flagged the week before**, and again in the week. Never the night before.
- **Every weekday line answers the same four questions:** who is out, who does drop-off, who does pickup, what is on afterwards.
- **If nobody covers a handoff, say so plainly.** Do not resolve it, do not assume the helper will. Raise it.

## Never

- **Never invent a time, an amount or an address.** If it is not written in a source, it does not appear.
- **Never repeat every email.** Only items carrying a date, an amount, a deadline or an instruction survive the sweep.
- **Never let an email's day-of-the-week mean this week.** An email that says "Monday" means the Monday after it was sent. If its deadline has already passed, drop it or mark it passed.
- **Never bury a fact inside a joke.** The joke can sit before or after the fact. Not on it.
- **Never cut a time, a place or an owner to make the message shorter.** Cut the commentary instead.

## Radar

- **One line.** The single most important thing in the next two weeks. Two lines only if two genuinely tie.
- Worth a radar line: camps, holidays, term boundaries, performances, anything needing money or a costume.
- Not worth it: routine assemblies, meetings you do not attend.

## Add your own

Every line below this point should be traceable to a specific week where the message was wrong.

*Write the rule, not the story. "Match the receipts before you nag" is a rule. "Remember that time it asked me to pay for the excursion twice" is a story, and a story cannot be followed.*

## Two real examples of a mistake becoming a rule

**The one that made the receipt rule.** The message asked me to pay for an excursion. I had paid for it the week before. The receipt had arrived from a different address to the one that asked, so as far as the system was concerned the request was still open.

> The rule: before asking for money, look for a matching confirmation from any address within three days either side. If there is one, mark it done.

**The one that made the date rule.** An email sent on a Thursday said "Monday is the dress rehearsal, contact the school tomorrow if you want your child left out of the photo". The message put that deadline on the following Monday. Both days had already passed by the time anybody read it.

> The rule: a day named in an email is relative to the day that email was sent, never to the week being written about.

Both of those are one line. Both took a wrong message to find. That is the whole method.
