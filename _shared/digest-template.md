# digest-template.md

The shape of the message.

Two versions of the same week. The text is the one the family actually reads. The email is the archive, and exists so that when somebody asks "what did it say about Thursday", there is an answer.

---

## The text

The primary artifact. Assume it is read standing up, one-handed, with somebody talking.

1. **Greeting.** One line, per `voice.md`.
2. **The week label.** "Week: Mon 6 to Sun 12 Oct".
3. **Day by day.** Only days with something on. Each day: who is out, who has drop-off and pickup, then what is on, with times exactly as the source wrote them. Anything urgent goes inside its day, not in a separate list at the bottom where it will be missed.
4. **A short reminders tail.** Two or three lines, genuinely urgent only. Deadlines, money, anything with a cap or a cut-off.
5. **Sign-off.** One line.

**A blank line between each day.** It sounds trivial. A wall of text on a phone does not get read, and the whole point is that it gets read.

**Under 1200 characters.** When it runs long, cut commentary. Never a time, a place or an owner.

## The email

The same week, with room to breathe.

1. **Greeting**, one line.
2. **Every day, Monday to Sunday**, including the quiet ones. A quiet day can be one line, but it should be there, because its absence reads as an oversight.
3. **REMEMBER.** Money, forms, bring-and-wear, bookings. Deadlines in bold. Items already paid marked done rather than deleted, so you can see it was considered.
4. **RADAR.** One line, per `rules.md`.
5. **Sign-off.**

## A filled-in example

Made up, and deliberately an ordinary week rather than a dramatic one.

> Evening, all.
>
> Week: Mon 6 to Sun 12 Oct.
>
> Mon: Both of us home. Swimming 4pm, the little one comes along.
>
> Tue: You in the office. Library books go back today. Guitar 5pm.
>
> Wed: Both of us in the city. Nobody has the 3:15 pickup yet, and the helper has the little one all day. Sort this one before Tuesday night.
>
> Thu: Home. Excursion form and $14 due today, online.
>
> Fri: Free dress day, gold coin. Football training 4:30pm at the far ground.
>
> Sat: Nothing on.
>
> Sun: Nothing on.
>
> Reminders: excursion $14 due Thursday. Free dress Friday, gold coin.
>
> Radar: term ends Friday week, then two weeks at home.

Read it again and notice what it does not do. It does not tell you the week is busy. It does not congratulate you for getting through it. Every line is a fact you would otherwise have to go and find.

## The test

Hand the text to somebody who is not in your house and ask them to run your Wednesday.

If they can, it works. If they ask you a question, the answer to that question is the next line in `rules.md`.
